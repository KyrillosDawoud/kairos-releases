Privacy policy

Last updated October 16, 2026

Kairos is a desktop app for Windows that brings your calendars, classes, money and tasks into one place. This policy says what Kairos reads, where it keeps it, who else sees it and how to delete it. It covers the Kairos app, Kairos's server and this website.

The short version

  • Kairos reads only the services you connect, and reads them only to show them to you.
  • Access to Google, Microsoft, Canvas and SimpleFIN Bridge stays on your computer, in Windows Credential Manager.
  • Kairos's server keeps your account, the tasks, events and notes you make in Kairos, tasks from Microsoft To Do if you turn it on, the applications and professional contacts you track, what Kairos remembers about you, the symbols on your Markets watchlist and the links to banks you connect through Plaid.
  • Your mail and Teams chats are read on your computer and never stored on Kairos's server. Kairos never sends mail or messages.
  • Your data goes to Anthropic only when you ask Kairos something: through Kairos AI on Kairos's server, or through your own Anthropic API key if you add one.
  • Your conversations with Kairos stay on your computer.
  • We do not sell your data, show ads or use your data to train AI models.
  • You can disconnect any service at any time and ask us to delete your account.

Who makes Kairos

Your Kairos account

You sign in to Kairos with an email address and password, or with your Google or Microsoft account.

  • Email and password. Kairos's server stores your email address and a one-way hash of your password. Nobody can read the password back.
  • Google or Microsoft. You sign in on Google's or Microsoft's own page. Kairos asks only for basic sign-in information: your email address and, where the provider shares them, your name and profile picture. Kairos's server stores these with your account. Signing in this way does not give Kairos your calendar or mail. Those are separate connections that you turn on yourself.
  • Profile. Your display name and time zone.

Your session is kept in Windows Credential Manager, so Kairos signs you in on the next start without asking.

What Kairos's server stores

Kairos's server runs on Supabase. It stores:

  • Your account, as described above.
  • What you make in Kairos: your tasks, the events you create in Kairos, your notes, your Kairos calendars and the links between them.
  • Your applications and network in Professional: each application you track (the firm, its site, the role, the place, the stage and each stage it reached with its date, the deadline, the next step, pay, notes and the link to the posting), the people you add (the name, firm, title, team, school, email and LinkedIn link you type, how you met and when to follow up), the touches you log with them and who is on which application. You can edit or delete each one. Kairos never reads LinkedIn.
  • What Kairos remembers about you: short sentences you add in Settings, Memory, and the ones Kairos saves as you talk, such as a goal or a routine, so it knows you on every device. You can edit or forget each one.
  • Your Markets watchlist: the ticker symbols you follow and their order, so the list is the same on every computer. Nothing else about a symbol, no amount and no account.
  • For each bank you connect through Plaid: the item id Plaid gives it, the bank's name, the access token Plaid issues for it and the last error code Plaid reported. Only Kairos's server function can read the access token. The app never receives it.
  • For each Kairos AI request, a usage record: which part of Kairos asked, the model, the number of tokens and the cost. Not the request and not the answer.
  • Your career profile, if you fill one in Professional, Profile: the facts the Kairos extension fills applications from, your answers to common questions, the voluntary self identification answers you chose to save and your resume, a PDF in a private store only your account can read.

Kairos's server also keeps a shared cache of public market data, such as a stock's price, a company's SEC filings and the Treasury's yields, so many people reading one symbol cost one request. It holds nothing about you or your account.

Each account's rows are protected by row level security, so only your signed-in account can read them. Events from Google Calendar, Outlook and calendar feeds, your mail, Teams chats and OneDrive files, your Canvas data, your balances and transactions, your conversations with Kairos and your Anthropic key are not stored on the server. Balances and transactions from Plaid, and Kairos AI requests and answers, pass through the server and are not saved there.

Like any web service, Supabase keeps request logs, including IP addresses, for a short time to run and protect the service.

What stays on your computer

Most of what Kairos reads never leaves your PC.

In Windows Credential Manager

Windows encrypts these for your Windows account:

  • Your Kairos session.
  • The refresh tokens for Google and Microsoft.
  • Your Canvas sign-in session or access token.
  • Your SimpleFIN Bridge access address.
  • The addresses of calendar feeds you link.
  • Your Anthropic API key. The app can save or remove it but cannot read it back.

In the app's storage

  • Caches of your events, tasks, connected calendars, Canvas work and money, so Kairos opens fast and works offline. Money is read again at most every 6 hours unless you choose Refresh.
  • What Kairos learns from how you use it: when you finish tasks, which tasks move to a later day, how long study blocks run, which suggestions you act on and which proposals from Claude you apply. Kairos keeps at most 3,000 of these signals, none older than 180 days. They are never sent to Kairos's server.
  • Your conversations with Kairos, the newest 50, with the files you attached, so you can reopen one where it ended. You can delete any of them, or all of them, on the Kairos screen.
  • Money category corrections, your net worth history, the names, order and notes you give your accounts, which accounts you hid, class times and settings.
  • Company icons on Money and Professional. Kairos asks each bank's, merchant's and firm's own website for its icon, the way a browser does, and keeps the answer for 30 days. That site learns only that a computer asked for its icon. No icon service is involved. Settings, Money, Show logos turns it off.
  • Job postings you paste in Professional. Kairos reads the start of that page from the posting's own site to fill in the firm, the role, the place and the closing date, and keeps nothing of the page. It never reads LinkedIn.

Signing out removes the cached copy of your Kairos data from the computer. Connections stay until you disconnect them. Settings, What Kairos has learned lets you remove any learned item, forget everything or turn learning off.

Reminders and the morning brief are made on your computer from these caches. They use Windows notifications, not a push service.

What each connection reads

Every connection is optional and turned on by you. All of them are read only except Google Calendar, where Kairos can create, change and delete events on calendars you can edit, when you ask it to, and Google Tasks, which Kairos keeps in step with your Kairos tasks. Kairos uses what it reads only to show it to you in Kairos and, when you ask, to answer with Claude.

Google Calendar

Reads
The list of calendars you can see and their events: title, time, location, description and video call link. Your email address, to show which account is connected.
Changes
When you ask, Kairos creates, changes or deletes an event in Google Calendar: when you save an event, drag or resize it, delete it, add one with quick add, or apply a change Kairos proposed. Only on calendars you own or can edit, and only the event you changed. Kairos never changes a calendar you can only view and never emails your guests about a change.
Access
openid, email, calendar.calendarlist.readonly and calendar.events. The last one lets Kairos read events and create, change and delete them. It does not let Kairos change your calendars, their sharing or your settings.
Kept
The refresh token in Credential Manager. Events in the app's cache on your computer.
Ends
Disconnect deletes the token and revokes Kairos's access at Google.

Google Tasks

Reads
Your task lists and their tasks: title, notes, due date and whether each is done.
Changes
Every 15 minutes while Tasks is on, Kairos keeps Google Tasks and your Kairos tasks in step: a task you add, change, finish or delete in one appears the same way in the other. Tasks you make in Kairos go to My Tasks. Tasks from Canvas stay in Kairos.
Access
tasks, asked for only when you turn Tasks on.
Kept
Each Google task becomes a Kairos task, stored with your account on Kairos's server like any task you make. Which Kairos task is which Google task is kept on your computer.
Ends
Turn Tasks off to stop the sync. Your tasks stay in both places. Disconnect revokes Kairos's access at Google.

Google Drive

Reads
Only the files you pick in Google's own picker: their names, types, dates and contents when you attach one or import a syllabus. Kairos cannot see any other file in your Drive.
Access
drive.file, asked for only when you turn Drive on. Kairos never changes or deletes a file.
Kept
Nothing from Drive is stored on Kairos's server. A file you attach goes into that conversation.
Ends
Turn Drive off or disconnect Google.

Google Classroom

Reads
Your active classes, their published work with your own submissions and grades, and announcements. Read only: Kairos never turns in work or posts.
Access
classroom.courses.readonly, classroom.coursework.me.readonly and classroom.announcements.readonly, asked for only when you turn Classroom on.
Kept
In the app's cache on your computer, beside Canvas. Not stored on Kairos's server.
Ends
Turn Classroom off or disconnect Google.

Gmail

When it is offered, Kairos will read the headers and the first words of recent inbox mail on your computer to find the few messages that ask something of you, will never send, delete or move mail, and will not store mail on Kairos's server. Outlook and Microsoft 365

One Microsoft account with five services. Each one turns on only when you turn it on, with its own approval at Microsoft.

Calendar
Your calendars and their events: subject, time, location, the first lines of the body and the online meeting link. Calendars.ReadWrite lets Kairos create, change and delete events on calendars you can edit, and add a Teams meeting to one, only when you ask.
Mail
The last 14 days of your inbox: sender, recipients, subject, the first lines Outlook gives and whether it was read. Kairos reads them on your computer to find the few that need you. When you ask Kairos about one, it reads that thread's text. Mail.Read cannot send, move or delete mail.
To Do
Your lists and tasks, kept the same as your Kairos tasks both ways with Tasks.ReadWrite. A task from To Do is stored with your other Kairos tasks on Kairos's server, with its id in To Do.
OneDrive
The names, sizes and folders of your recent files and the ones you search for, and the contents of a file only when you pick it. Files.Read cannot change or delete a file.
Teams chat
Your chats with the last message of each, on a work or school account, to find the ones waiting on your reply, and one chat's messages when you ask Kairos about it. Chat.Read cannot send a message or read a team's channels.
Access
Always openid, email, offline_access and User.Read, which is your name and email address. Then only the permissions of the services you turn on.
Kept
The refresh token in Credential Manager. Events, mail, chats and file lists in the app's cache on your computer, never on Kairos's server. Tasks from To Do, as above.
Ends
Turning a service off stops its reads. Disconnect deletes the token. Remove the approval at Microsoft as well.

Access

Kept
The school's session cookies or the token in Credential Manager. Your Canvas data in the app's cache on your computer.
Ends
Disconnect deletes the session or token from your computer.

University of Virginia class search

The Kairos extension for Chrome and Edge

The extension fills job applications from the career profile you keep in Kairos, under Professional, Profile. It runs only on application pages at Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workday, iCIMS, SmartRecruiters and Oracle Taleo. Its permissions are storage, activeTab, scripting and those seven sites. It has no access to any other site.

Reads
On those sites, the labels and names of the form's fields, the job's title, company and location, and the page's address. On any other page, the same job details only when you open the extension there. It never reads what you type, your other tabs or your browsing history.
Sends
Nothing leaves the page except two things you ask for. Fill puts your profile's values into that page's form. Save to Kairos and Mark applied write one row to Applications in your Kairos account: the company, the role, the location, the page's address and the date. A draft you ask for sends the question, the company, the role and your saved answers to Claude through Kairos AI, as described below. Your name, email and phone are never in it.
Never
The extension never presses Submit and never fills a field before you press Fill. It answers a voluntary self identification question, such as gender, race, veteran status or disability, only with the answer you saved for it in Kairos, and otherwise leaves it empty. It never answers a question about a Social Security number, a birth date, pay or criminal history, and it never signs or agrees to anything for you. It sells nothing, shows no ads and sends nothing to an analytics service.
Kept
Your sign in lives in the browser's session storage for the extension, which empties when the browser closes. Your profile and resume stay in your Kairos account. Your resume is in a private store that only your account can read, as a PDF of 5 MB at most.
Ends
Sign out in the extension, or remove it from the browser. Delete account in Kairos deletes your profile, your resume and your applications.

Claude and Anthropic

Kairos's assistant runs on Claude, made by Anthropic, in one of two ways:

  • Kairos AI, when you are signed in. A request goes from your computer to Kairos's server, which checks your session and sends it to Anthropic under Kairos's own Anthropic account, then streams the answer back. The server keeps only the usage record described above. Each account can use up to $3 of Kairos AI a month.
  • Your own key, if you add an Anthropic API key in Settings. It is kept in Credential Manager and sent only to Anthropic's Messages API. Each request goes from your computer straight to Anthropic, under your own Anthropic account, and Kairos's server is not involved.

When you ask Kairos something, it sends:

  • Your question and, in a conversation, the earlier questions and answers.
  • Your events for the next 14 days: title, time, calendar name and location.
  • Your open tasks with their due dates.
  • Canvas work due in the next 14 days, with its class and whether you submitted it.
  • What Kairos remembers about you, the patterns it measured in your calendar and tasks and what it has learned about your schedule.
  • For a planning question, the free time Kairos found in your calendar.
  • On the Kairos screen, more when a question needs it: other days of your calendar, tasks, search results from your events, tasks and notes, class work, your applications and professional contacts with their touches and, for a money question, money as described below.

Only in these cases, and only for that request:

  • Money goes only with a question about money: net worth, this month and last month, accounts and balances, recurring charges, charges worth a look and up to 150 transactions from the last 60 days.
  • A picture of your screen goes only with a question you ask about it: when you hold Ctrl Alt Space to ask the companion, turn on Look at my screen or press Ctrl Alt S. It is one picture of the screen under your pointer, scaled down and sent with that question. With a question to the companion goes the name of the app in front and its window title, such as the page or the document open in it; without a picture neither goes. When the window in front looks private, a password manager or a page whose title says sign in, a password, a code or a card, Kairos takes no picture and sends neither. When Kairos points at something, a zoomed part of the same picture goes once more. It is never saved to disk, and the companion says when a picture goes. Turn off Read my screen when I ask in Settings, Companion, and Kairos takes no picture at all. On an application form Claude names only which field is next. Your saved value stays on your computer: Fill from my profile copies it, or hands it to the Kairos extension, which fills the field and never submits the form.
  • A syllabus goes when you import one: the PDF you pick, from your computer or OneDrive, the course name and the term dates.
  • Mail and Teams chats go only with a question about them: the few that need you, as sender, subject, why and when, and the text of one thread or chat when Kairos reads it to answer. Kairos never sends mail or messages.
  • The morning brief, if you turn on the brief by Claude: today's events, tasks due or overdue, what needs you and your focus hours.
  • Files you attach: the images and PDFs you add to a message on the Kairos screen, from your computer or OneDrive, with that message and the later ones in the same conversation.
  • Web search and web pages: Claude may search the web and read a page through Anthropic to answer a question.
  • After a conversation, unless learning is off: what each side said, once more, so Claude can update what Kairos remembers about you. Files and tool results are not sent again.

Claude proposes changes to your events and tasks. Nothing there changes until you apply them, and Claude can never change events that come from Google, Outlook or a feed. In Professional, when you ask, Claude adds an application, moves one to a stage or logs a touch, and says so; each one shows in Professional, where you can change or delete it. Claude writes email drafts for you to send yourself. Kairos never sends mail.

Anthropic handles these requests under its own terms with you. By Anthropic's published policy, it does not use inputs and outputs from its API to train its models by default, and it deletes them within 30 days, with the exceptions it lists. See Is my data used for model training? and How long do you store my organization's data? Kairos does not send your data to any other AI provider. If that changes, this policy will name the provider first, and Settings, Kairos AI will show it.

Google user data

Kairos's use of information received from Google APIs will adhere to Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

The use of information received from Google Workspace scopes will adhere to the Google User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

In particular:

  • What Kairos reads. With Google Calendar: your calendar list and events, as listed above. With Google Tasks: your lists and tasks. With Google Drive: only the files you pick. With Google Classroom: your classes, work, grades and announcements. With Sign in with Google: your email address, name and profile picture.
  • What Kairos changes. With Google Calendar: the events you create, change or delete in Kairos, on calendars you can edit, only when you ask. With Google Tasks: the tasks you add, change, finish or delete in Kairos, while Tasks is on. Nothing else.
  • How it is used. Only to show your calendars in the calendar, reminders, Today and Up next, and to make the changes you ask for. When you ask Kairos a question, also to answer it. Your sign-in details identify your Kairos account.
  • Where it is kept. Calendar, Drive and Classroom data stays on your computer, in the app's cache and in your conversations with Kairos, and is never stored on Kairos's server. Google tasks become Kairos tasks, stored with your account on Kairos's server. Sign-in details are stored with your account on Kairos's server.
  • Who it goes to. Google data goes to Anthropic only when you ask Kairos a question, as part of that request, through Kairos AI or your own key. Kairos AI passes it through Kairos's server without saving it. It goes to no one else.
  • Never. Google user data is never sold, never used for advertising, never used to decide credit or lending, never given to data brokers and never used to create, train or improve any AI or machine learning model.
  • People. Nobody reads your Google data. The only exceptions: you ask us to in a support request, security needs it (for example, to investigate abuse) or the law requires it.
  • Deleting it. Disconnect Google in Kairos to delete the token and revoke access, or remove Kairos at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Signing out removes the cached events.

  • We do not show ads or use your data for advertising.
  • We do not share your data with data brokers or use it to decide credit or lending.
  • We do not use your data to train AI models, ours or anyone else's.

  • Nobody at YOUR NAME OR COMPANY reads your data unless you ask us to in a support request, it is needed for security or the law requires it.

Who else handles your data

Kairos relies on these services. Each one receives only what its part needs.

  • Supabase runs Kairos's server: your account, sign-in emails and the data listed under What Kairos's server stores.
  • Anthropic runs Claude, only when you ask: under Kairos's Anthropic account for Kairos AI, or under your own if you add your key.

Kairos's server asks Tiingo for the ticker symbols you open, follow or hold, and nothing else: not your name, your account or how much you hold. The SEC, Nasdaq Trader and the US Treasury provide the list of securities, company filings and yields, which the server reads as public data.
  • Google, Microsoft and Instructure (Canvas, through your school) provide the data you connect and the sign-ins you choose.
  • GitHub hosts the installer, the updates Kairos checks for and this website. Like any web host, it sees your IP address when you download or Kairos checks for an update.

We may share data when the law requires it or to protect the safety of people or the service. If Kairos is ever sold or merged, we will ask for your consent before Google user data moves to the new owner.

These services may process data in the United States and in other countries.

Keeping and deleting data

  • On your computer. Caches last until you sign out, disconnect the service or uninstall Kairos. Learned signals are dropped after 180 days. Conversations with Kairos last until you delete them, and only the newest 50 are kept.
  • On the server. Your account and what you make in Kairos are kept while your account exists. When you delete a task, event or note, Kairos marks it deleted and hides it everywhere. It is removed for good when your account is deleted. A memory you forget is deleted at once. Kairos AI usage records are kept while your account exists.
  • Plaid links. Kept until you remove the bank or your account is deleted.
  • Watchlist. A symbol you remove is deleted at once. The list is deleted with your account.
  • At Anthropic. Under Anthropic's policy, linked above.

To delete your account, open Kairos, Settings, Account, Delete account. Kairos ends every bank link at Plaid, deletes your account with everything Kairos's server holds for it, disconnects your connections and clears what it keeps for you on that computer. You can also write to privacy@YOUR-DOMAIN from the email address on the account, and we do the same within 30 days and write back when it is done. Support says how to clear another computer.

Security

  • Every connection uses HTTPS.
  • Tokens and keys on your computer are kept in Windows Credential Manager, which Windows encrypts for your Windows account. They are never written to a file in plain text.
  • Kairos's server never holds your access to Google, Microsoft, Canvas or SimpleFIN Bridge. Plaid access tokens are readable only by Kairos's server function.
  • Supabase encrypts the database at rest. Row level security keeps each account's rows private to that account.
  • Updates are signed, and Kairos installs only an update whose signature checks out.

No system is perfectly secure. If we learn of a breach that affects your data, we will tell you without undue delay.
